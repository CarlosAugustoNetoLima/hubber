Readiness

The 10 days only start when this is in place.

The countdown does not run while any of these pieces is missing. Promising otherwise would be selling a deadline that cannot be met.

  • Signed contract and confirmed first payment.
  • Access to accounts, tools and data.
  • A decision maker available to approve within the week.
  • Usable inputs: brand, product, pricing and proof.
  • Someone on your side responsible for answering the lead.

That last one is not a detail. Generating demand for an operation that does not answer is the most expensive way to find out the constraint was somewhere else.

Questions

What people ask before starting.

What begins during the 10 business days?

The whole operation: understanding the business, positioning, campaign architecture, production, measurement and launch. By the tenth day there is a campaign live, a dashboard built and the next cycle planned. What the 10 days do not do is deliver mature results: that is the work of the cycles that follow.

What is included in our scope?

The two fronts describe the capabilities available, but scope is always specific: which channels, what production volume, how many pages, what cadence. That is defined in the diagnostic and recorded in the contract, with what is in and what is out. We do not work with open ended promises like all channels or unlimited revisions.

How are approvals and reporting managed?

Approvals have a workflow and a deadline defined during onboarding, so they do not become the bottleneck. Reporting is not a monthly PDF: visibility is weekly, on tasks, risks and blockers, and the business reading happens in the monthly executive review.

Who owns sales follow-up?

Your team. Hubber hands over the lead with context, organizes the handoff into the CRM and monitors the journey checkpoints, but the people who answer and close are your sales team. That is why the readiness gate requires someone responsible for responding.

What is the minimum engagement term?

Defined in the contract, case by case. What we can say up front is the logic: an operation tied to pipeline needs more than one cycle to produce a reliable signal, and we agree that horizon before starting, not after.

Do you guarantee results?

No. We guarantee scope, method and transparency: every decision recorded, every risk flagged early, every number with an owner. Results also depend on what your company delivers: access, budget, commercial capacity and approvals on time. That is why the readiness gate exists.

Can Agency become Build later?

It can, and it is a common path. Agency runs the operation while the company does not yet have its own structure; when bringing it in house makes sense, Build creates the department with the operating history already in hand. The reverse happens too, with Agency taking on fronts the internal team will not cover.
